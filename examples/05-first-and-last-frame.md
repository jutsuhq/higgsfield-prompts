# 05 · Get from one still to another

**Request:** "I have the abandoned greenhouse and the same greenhouse in full bloom. Make it transform."

| Tag | File |
|---|---|
| @Image1 | `greenhouse-dead.png` |
| @Image2 | `greenhouse-bloom.png` (same aspect ratio) |

**Settings:** omni-reference, 16:9, 10 s.

```
@Image1 is the first frame. It defines the opening composition: the abandoned Victorian greenhouse with broken panes, dry dead stems and grey dust.
@Image2 is the last frame. It defines the ending composition: the same greenhouse from the same camera position, its panes repaired and every bed overflowing with flowers in warm sunlight.

One continuous transformation from the first frame to the last. The camera stays locked off. A beam of sunlight breaks through the roof; green shoots push up through the dust, stems thicken and climb the iron frame, buds swell and open in waves from the front beds to the back, cracked panes fill with clear glass, and the light warms from grey to gold. The video arrives exactly at the last frame.
Audio: <glass chimes softly as the panes mend> <leaves rustle as the stems climb> under a rising hum of bees. No music, no dialogue.
Locked camera throughout. No people, no text, no logos.
```

**Why it works**

- The action accounts for every difference between the two frames (light, plants, glass). Any difference not described is where the clip arrives "near" the last frame instead of at it.
- The camera is locked, so all the change happens in the scene.
- Both frames share an aspect ratio. A mismatched last frame gets stretched.
